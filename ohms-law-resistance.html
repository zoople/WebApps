Picturing Resistance

Why R = V ÷ I, and why more flow at the same push means less resistance

Read R = V / I as "volts per amp": how much push it takes to get each amp of flow.

It's a rate, like dollars per kilo or km per hour. At a fixed voltage, more current means less resistance. You're right about that.

The part that usually causes confusion

Resistance belongs to the pipe itself. Voltage and current are only how you measure it. The formula doesn't say resistance is caused by volts and amps. It says you can work out how narrow the pipe is by comparing the push you applied to the flow you got.

So the same relationship gets rearranged depending on what you already know:

  • I = V / R: with a given push through a given pipe, this is the flow you get.
  • V = I × R: to get a given flow through a given pipe, this is the push you need. A narrower pipe needs more pressure.
  • R = V / I: if you saw this push produce this flow, this is how narrow the pipe must be.

It's one relationship viewed from three sides.
