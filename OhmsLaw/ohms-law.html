Picturing Resistance

Why R = V ÷ I, and why more flow at the same push means less resistance
Read R = V / I as "volts per amp": the push needed for each amp of flow.
  • It's a rate, like dollars per kilo or km per hour.
  • At a fixed voltage, more current means less resistance.

The part that usually causes confusion

  • Resistance belongs to the pipe itself.
  • Voltage and current are only how you measure it.
  • Compare the push you applied with the flow you got, and you know how narrow the pipe is.

One relationship, three sides

  • I = V / R: this push through this pipe gives this flow.
  • V = I × R: this flow through this pipe needs this push. Narrower pipe, more pressure.
  • R = V / I: this push gave this flow, so the pipe must be this narrow.
